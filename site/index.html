      What is actually going on

      It was built to look right, not to do a job.

      Nearly every website we are asked to fix was signed off on how it looked. Somewhere in that process, nobody wrote down what it was actually supposed to achieve, or how this particular business wins work. So it was never pointed at anything.

      What was agreed

      • Colours and fonts
      • How the pages look
      • Which photographs to use
      • How many pages there would be
      • A date to go live

      How we work

      We start with how your business actually works.

      Before a single page is designed, we want to understand the job the website is supposed to do. That is the part almost everybody skips, and it is the part that decides whether the thing ever earns its keep.

      01

      We learn how you win work

      Who actually buys from you, what they ask first, what makes them hesitate, and what finally makes them say yes. In your words, not in ours. You will know all of this already. It has simply never been written down.

      02

      We find what is getting in the way

      Then we go through the website looking for every place it makes that harder than it needs to be. There are always more of them than anyone expects, and they are rarely the things people worry about.

      03

      We take those away

      And rebuild around what is left. Nothing gets added because it is fashionable, and nothing stays because it was expensive. The test is simple: does it help somebody decide to work with you.

      04

      You see it as it happens

      You approve the work in stages as it goes up, so there is never a reveal at the end that you are quietly unsure about and too far in to question.

      Eighteen years of doing this has taught us one thing above all: the website is almost never the first problem to solve.

      What is changing

      How to be chosen when the machine does the comparing.

      People used to open ten websites and compare them over a week. More and more, they now ask an assistant who to use, and it does the reading for them. Finding you and judging you have become the same moment.

      1. It reads what your site says, not how it looks.
      2. It compares you with everyone else in a few seconds.
      3. It passes on the businesses that answer the question plainly.

      A website built around how you win work gives a person and a machine the same thing: a reason to choose you.

      How the rebuild works

      We read everything first. Then we rebuild it.

      Most rebuilds stall because everyone tries to rewrite the company at the same time. We do not. Your content is the starting point, not the obstacle.

      01

      We read the whole site

      Every page, every PDF, every product line, every accreditation buried three clicks deep. Nothing is summarised by guesswork. The things that matter commercially are usually the things the old site hid.

      02

      We rebuild to a modern standard

      Clean, fast, accessible, machine-readable markup with measurement and consent in place from the first page. Your existing structure is kept unless there is a reason to change it, so your team is not relearning their own website.

      03

      You approve it in stages

      Sections go up on a staging URL as they are finished. You comment, we adjust, we move on. No eight-week silence followed by one nervous reveal.

      04

      It goes live, and we keep going

      Launch is a day, not a finish line. Post-launch changes continue on the same rhythm, which is how a site stays current instead of ageing into the next rebuild.

      Questions

      Plain answers

      Can you work alongside our existing agency?

      Yes, and it is often the better arrangement. The agency keeps running campaigns while we audit account structure, attribution and landing pages. We report findings to both sides, so your agency receives a brief rather than a threat. Where an account genuinely needs rebuilding from the ground up we can take it over, but we do not push for that unless the numbers justify it.

      How long does a corporate rebuild take?

      For a typical corporate site of twenty to sixty pages, six weeks from kickoff to live is normal, with sections staged for approval as they are built. Keeping your existing content and structure removes most of the delay, which usually comes from trying to rewrite the whole company at once.

      What does it cost?

      Across the SA market in 2026, paid media management runs from roughly R12,000 to R45,000 a month for accounts spending R75,000 to R500,000, with once-off audits between R8,000 and R25,000. Percentage-of-spend models are common, but they reward spending more rather than spending better, so we do not use one. We scope each engagement after the audit, and you get a written figure within one business day of the call.

      Do you rebuild sites you did not build?

      That is most of the work. Corporate sites built five to fifteen years ago on dated platforms, where the content is still sound but the platform is holding the business back. Existing content and imagery are carried across wherever they still serve.

      Who actually does the work?

      A small senior team, and the person on your first call stays on the account. We take a limited number of engagements each quarter so that senior time stays on the work rather than on managing juniors.

      How does this sit with POPIA?

      Every tracking, consent and form setup we install is built to comply with POPIA and Section 69 consent rules from the start. All data stays in accounts you own, and our own enquiry form stores no raw IP addresses.
